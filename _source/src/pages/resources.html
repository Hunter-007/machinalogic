01 · Primer

What is operational technology?

Operational technology (OT) is the hardware and software that monitors and controls physical processes: the controllers that open valves, the systems that balance a power grid, the screens operators use to run a treatment works. Industrial control systems (ICS), including SCADA and distributed control systems (DCS), are the core of OT.

For decades, these systems were isolated from the outside world. That is no longer true. Remote monitoring, cloud analytics, vendor support and business reporting have connected OT to corporate networks and the internet, often without the security architecture to match.

Many of these systems were designed for reliability, not security. Common industrial protocols such as Modbus were created without authentication, and controllers often run for decades on software that can't be easily patched. Securing them requires a different approach from corporate IT.

02 · Architecture

The Purdue model, in one view.

The Purdue reference model organises an industrial network into levels, from physical process at the bottom to enterprise IT at the top. It's the common language for designing segmentation and deciding what should talk to what.

Enterprise zone (IT)
L5 / L4Enterprise & businessEmail, ERP, business planning, internet access, corporate data centre and cloud
Demilitarised zone
L3.5IT/OT DMZBrokered data exchange: jump hosts, historian replicas, patch and antivirus staging. No direct IT-to-control traffic.
Manufacturing / control zone (OT)
L3Site operationsHistorians, engineering workstations, site-wide supervisory and production management
L2Supervisory controlHMIs, SCADA servers and operator workstations for an area or process
L1Basic controlPLCs, RTUs, DCS controllers and safety instrumented systems that execute control logic
L0Physical processSensors, actuators, motors, valves and drives that touch the physical world

Modern environments with cloud services, IIoT devices and wireless links don't always fit neatly into these levels, but the model remains the most widely used starting point for segmentation design.

03 · Differences

Why IT security doesn't transfer directly.

The same tools and habits that protect an office network can, applied carelessly, stop a production line.

Corporate ITOperational technology
First priorityConfidentiality of dataSafety and availability of the physical process
PatchingDays to weeks, often automatedMonths to years, tied to planned outages and vendor approval
Asset lifespan3 to 5 years15 to 30 years, often running legacy operating systems
ProtocolsModern, usually encrypted and authenticatedIndustrial protocols, many without built-in authentication

Downtime toleranceMaintenance windows are routineContinuous operation; unplanned downtime is costly or dangerous

04 · Frameworks

The frameworks that matter for OT.

No single standard covers everything. These are the references most organisations use to structure an OT security programme.

INTERNATIONAL STANDARD

ISA/IEC 62443

A series of standards for the security of industrial automation and control systems, covering asset owners, system integrators and product suppliers. Introduces key concepts such as zones and conduits and security levels.

GUIDANCE

NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3

The US National Institute of Standards and Technology's Guide to Operational Technology Security, updated in 2023. Widely used worldwide as a practical reference for securing OT environments.

MANAGEMENT SYSTEM

ISO/IEC 27001

The international standard for information security management systems. Designed for IT, but often extended to cover OT governance when combined with IEC 62443.

SECTOR REFERENCE

NERC CIP

Mandatory cybersecurity standards for the bulk electric system in North America. Not applicable in Africa, but a useful reference model for utilities and energy regulators.

06 · Glossary

OT terms, in plain language.

PLC Programmable logic controller
A rugged industrial computer that runs control logic, reading sensors and driving actuators such as motors and valves.
RTU Remote terminal unit
A field device that collects data and executes commands at remote sites, such as substations or pumping stations.
HMI Human-machine interface
The screens and software operators use to view and control a process.
SCADA
Supervisory control and data acquisition: systems that monitor and control geographically dispersed assets such as pipelines and grids.
DCS Distributed control system
An integrated control system common in continuous processes such as refining, chemicals and power generation.
SIS Safety instrumented system
An independent system that brings a process to a safe state when dangerous conditions are detected. The last line of defence.
Historian
A database that records process data over time, often bridging OT data to business users.
Engineering workstation
A computer used to program and configure controllers. A high-value target because it can change control logic.
IT/OT DMZ
A buffer network between corporate IT and OT, so that no traffic passes directly between the two.
Zones & conduits
An IEC 62443 concept: group assets with similar security needs into zones, and tightly control communication between them through conduits.
